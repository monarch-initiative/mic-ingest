Riboflavin, in its cofactor forms flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD) and flavin
mononucleotide (FMN), plays fundamental roles in energy metabolism, cellular
antioxidant potential, and metabolic interactions with other micronutrients,
including iron, vitamin B6, and folate. Severe riboflavin deficiency, largely
confined to low-income countries, clinically manifests as cheilosis, angular
stomatitis, glossitis, seborrheic dermatitis, and severe anemia with erythroid
hypoplasia. Subclinical deficiency may be much more widespread, including in
high-income countries, but typically goes undetected because riboflavin
biomarkers are rarely measured in human studies. There are adverse health
consequences of low and deficient riboflavin status throughout the life cycle,
including anemia and hypertension, that could contribute substantially to the
global burden of disease. This review considers the available evidence on
causes, detection, and consequences of riboflavin deficiency, ranging from
clinical deficiency signs to manifestations associated with less severe
deficiency, and the related research, public health, and policy priorities.
