New Evidence against Chromium as an Essential Trace Element.

Vincent JB(1).

Author information:
(1)Department of Chemistry, The University of Alabama, Tuscaloosa, AL 
jvincent@ua.edu.

Nearly 60 y ago, chromium, as the trivalent ion, was proposed to be an essential 
element, but the results of new studies indicate that chromium currently can 
only be considered pharmacologically active and not an essential element. 
Regardless, articles still continue to appear in the literature claiming 
chromium is an essential element. Chromium has been marketed as an agent to 
reduce body mass and develop muscle; however, such marketing claims are no 
longer allowed in the United States because these claims, similar to claims of 
essential status, are not supported by experiments. Trivalent chromium has also 
been proposed as a therapeutic agent to increase insulin sensitivity and affect 
lipid metabolism. Although effective in certain rodent models, beneficial 
effects in humans have not been unequivocally established. Molecular mechanisms 
have been proposed for the beneficial effects but have not been definitively 
shown to occur in animals.
