Comment in
    Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol. 2015 May;16(5):265. doi: 10.1038/nrm3992.

Lysine acetylation is a conserved protein post-translational modification that 
links acetyl-coenzyme A metabolism and cellular signalling. Recent advances in 
the identification and quantification of lysine acetylation by mass spectrometry 
have increased our understanding of lysine acetylation, implicating it in many 
biological processes through the regulation of protein interactions, activity 
and localization. In addition, proteins are frequently modified by other types 
of acylations, such as formylation, butyrylation, propionylation, succinylation, 
malonylation, myristoylation, glutarylation and crotonylation. The intricate 
link between lysine acylation and cellular metabolism has been clarified by the 
occurrence of several such metabolite-sensitive acylations and their selective 
removal by sirtuin deacylases. These emerging findings point to new functions 
for different lysine acylations and deacylating enzymes and also highlight the 
mechanisms by which acetylation regulates various cellular processes.
