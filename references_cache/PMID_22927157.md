# Caffeine and risk of Parkinson's disease in a large cohort of men and women.

Caffeine consumption has been associated with a reduced risk of Parkinson's 
disease (PD). The association is strong and consistent in men, but uncertain in 
women, possibly because of an interaction with hormone replacement therapy 
(HRT). We sought to confirm these findings using data on PD incidence in the 
Cancer Prevention Study II Nutrition Cohort (CPS II-Nutrition), a large, 
prospective study of men and women. We conducted a prospective study of caffeine 
intake and risk of PD within the CPS II Nutrition Cohort. Intakes of coffee and 
other sources of caffeine were assessed at baseline. Incident cases of PD (n = 
317; 197 men and 120 women) were confirmed by treating physicians and medical 
record review. Relative risks (RRs) were estimated using proportional hazards 
models, adjusting for age, smoking, and alcohol consumption. After adjustment 
for age, smoking, and alcohol intake, high caffeine consumption was associated 
with a reduced risk of PD. The RR comparing the 5th to the 1st quintile of 
caffeine intake was 0.43 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.26, 0.71; P trend = 
<0.002) in men, and 0.61 (95% CI: 0.34, 1.09; P trend = 0.05) in women. Among 
women, this association was stronger among never users of HRT (RR = 0.32) than 
among ever users (RR = 0.81; P interaction = 0.15). Consumption of decaffeinated 
coffee was not associated with PD risk. Findings from this large, prospective 
study of men and women are consistent with a protective effect of caffeine 
intake on PD incidence, with an attenuating influence of HRT in women. © 2012
